First, a correction to my wording. "Band" is already taken in this project: it means the horizontal strips inside a room (the 3–4 drawn bands). What I meant was a band of levels, such as levels 1–4, 5–8 and so on. That was confusing, and I should have said "per level group".

The idea

Each group of levels gets its own look and a small set of rules, so the 20 levels don't feel like one cave repeated. A group could change:
- Palette: the wall color (COLUPF), the background color (COLUBK), and the per-band color gradient the kernel already stages. For example, brown cave, then icy blue, then red lava, then dark green flooded.
- Hazard mix: the generator would weight hot rocks, water, crushable phase walls, bats and miners differently per group. Lava levels get more hot cells, and flooded levels get more rafts and water.
- Mechanics: a group could add a rule, such as the lamp/dark-room only in the deep levels.
- Music or SFX: a different tune per group, if banks 5–7 get used for audio.

Why it fits this project

- Mostly data, not kernel. Colors are baked in by convert_level.py from the level data, so a theme is mainly a palette table plus generator weights. I haven't traced exactly where per-level colors are stored, so I can't give a ROM cost yet.
- The generator is the product, so "theme = a set of generator parameters" fits your existing rule. A theme would be a named profile in Build20LevelStage, with its own test in test_generator.py.
- PAL is already handled: gen_colors.py maps NTSC hues to PAL hues and keeps the luma nibble. New theme colors must follow that rule, because the kernel reads luma bits (for example, ENAM0 reads bit 1 of the player color).
- No kernel or HERO-rule conflict: it only changes the values fed into the existing kernel.

Risks

- Luma constraints: the kernel reads some luma bits, so not every color is allowed.
- Readability: a new palette must keep walls, enemies and hazards distinguishable. Your user review of rendered stages would be needed.
- Frozen test data: themed colors would change the output, so the frozen test banks would need re-freezing.

If you want, I can check how level colors flow from the JSON to the ROM and tell you the actual cost.
=====================================================================================================================================================================

What the overlays do today

- Ball: it is 8 color clocks wide, which is exactly 1 cell on the 10-column grid.
  - It sits at one X for the whole cave frame, and the plan says HERO never repositions it inside the cave.
  - ENABL (the ball enable register) is gated per band on the setup lines.
  - The playfield priority bit (PFP) hides the ball wherever it overlaps a real wall.
- M1: it is a second patch, because the laser uses M0. The plan lists it as "parked", and Phase 3 would wire it in.
- Limits: patches are additive-only, so they can add a wall piece but not open a gap in a wall. They are also limited to about 1–2 cells and one X per object per frame.

Where there's room to use them better

1. Animate the X between frames.
   - The ball and M1 positions are set in the vertical blank (VBL), so changing them each frame costs no extra kernel cycles.
   - That gives sliding doors, a drifting wall, or a moving raft platform that is drawn by the hardware. Collision would have to follow the same X.
   - This links to the moving-raft idea from earlier.
2. Use both objects, with different widths.
   - The ball can be 1, 2, 4 or 8 clocks wide through CTRLPF. M1 can be 1, 2, 4 or 8 clocks through NUSIZ1.
   - Two patches per room, or a 1.5- or 2-cell patch, would allow more room shapes than one ball.
3. Use the band gating for taller and shorter shapes.
   - The ENABL value per band already lets one column show on any subset of the 4 bands.
   - A catalog of "which band combinations" would give stepped ledges and L-shaped walls from the same hardware.
4. Carve instead of only add.
   - The ball is colored from COLUPF, so it can only paint wall color. M1 is colored from COLUP1, which could be set to the background color to open a gap in a wall.
   - The plan notes that idle P1 could write wall defaults, marked unverified. This would be the most valuable change, but it needs a probe to confirm.
5. Color the patch differently.
   - The ball takes COLUPF, which is already staged per band. A patch on a hot band can look hot, with no extra cost.
6. Share patch layouts across rooms.
   - Patches are part of the per-level extras stream.
   - A small library of vetted patch types (door, ledge, pillar) would let the generator pick from them. That is where "more varied caves" would come from, and it fits the generator-as-product approach.

What the overlays can't do

They won't give a free-form right half. The late PF write that would do that is the dead approach from before, so the variety here is limited to a few extra shapes per room.

So for 2.0, I'd drop "asymmetric PF" as a goal. Better ways to get more varied caves within the current approach would be:
- more shape archetypes;
- better use of the overlay objects;
- hazard and enemy variety;
- theming per band.

Here are ideas for 2.0.0, grouped by theme. I only skimmed the repo. It looks like banks 5–7 are nearly empty (about 1 KB each) and the raft is still static, so that's where the headroom is.

Gameplay (the "2.0" feel)

- Moving raft. The raft is a static platform today, and your notes say motion was never specified. A drifting or rideable raft would make water rooms a real mechanic. It needs your ruling first.
- New enemy types. Candidates are a ceiling dripper, a rolling boulder, a spitting plant, or a mini-boss at the end of each band. Bats and miners are dodgeable, so a "must use the bomb" enemy would add variety.
- Boss rooms. Every N levels, a fixed hand-authored room with a pattern-based boss. It gives the generator a break and gives players a milestone.
- Pickups. Ideas are a power-bar refill, a temporary shield, a slow-fall or double-jump item, and a laser upgrade (wider beam or faster recharge).
- Hot rock wall and lamp/dark-room. You have plans for both (hot_rock_wall_plan.md, lamp_dark_room_plan.md). Shipping them as headline 2.0 features would make sense.
- Difficulty modes. Use the console difficulty switches or Game Select for Novice/Expert, with fewer or more enemies, a shorter fuse, and less power.
- Two-player alternate. Players swap on death, with separate scores, using the standard 2600 pattern.

Content / generator

- Endless mode. The generator is the product, so a seeded run with a displayed seed would give the game replay value.
- Themed bands. Every few levels would get a visual theme (ice, lava, flooded) with its own palette and hazard rules.
- Daily seed. Players could compare scores on the same seed.
- Secret rooms. Bomb-able walls that lead to bonus rooms.

Presentation

- Music and SFX. docs/2600_music_guide.txt already exists. Add a title jingle, a per-theme loop and a death sting, and use the free banks for the tunes.
- Better title/attract mode. The attract mode could replay a bot run, since you already have a bot and sim.
- Level-complete bonus tally. Show time, power left and lives as a score breakdown, as HERO does.
- Use the new art. art/logo.png and art/poster.png are untracked. A 2.0 box and label release could go with the manual PDF.

Technical

- Asymmetric playfield. asymmetric_pf_plan.md is large. Dropping the reflected-PF limit would allow far more varied caves, but it conflicts with the "follow HERO" rule, so it needs a deliberate decision.
- Fill the HUD gaps. The ball (ENABL) indicators and the 13+2 sprite text are still on your not-yet-implemented list.
- Flicker budget. Do another pass on the flicker findings so 2.0 has zero visible flicker with the extra enemies.
- Save and high scores. Use AtariVox/SaveKey support for high-score persistence. It's optional hardware, but it would be a good extra.
- Region support. An NTSC/PAL60/PAL build flag, with the frame timing parameterized (262 vs 312 scanlines).

Release / process

- Regression gates. Keep sim_* and qa_* as hard CI gates, and add a "full-run bot completes N levels" gate for the generator.
- Cartridge and manual. Publish a version with a ROM label, a manual update and a changelog.

If I had to pick a minimal set for a convincing 2.0, I'd take the moving raft, one or two new enemies, boss rooms, music and endless seeded mode. That gives new mechanics, new content, and replay value without a kernel rewrite.

Análise direta e honesta do código de Savior comparado ao disassembly de H.E.R.O. (disponível em docs/hero/ e hero/hero.bin):
  ──────
  ### Veredito: É uma recriação inspirada na arquitetura e design, NÃO uma cópia de código (ROM clone / copy-paste).

  O código-fonte de Savior foi escrito do zero, com convenções modernas (DASM estruturado com nomes descritivos, rotinas modulares, lógica de subpixel própria), mas
  reproduz deliberadamente as regras de design, limites físicos e decisões técnicas da engenharia de H.E.R.O.

  Abaixo estão os fatos técnicos comprovando isso:
  ──────
  ### 1. Onde as implementações são completamente diferentes (Código próprio)

  • O Kernel de Renderização:
      • H.E.R.O. (hero_bank0.asm / hero_bank1.asm):
      Utiliza escrita em registradores de Playfield linha a linha via tabelas de lookup compactadas na ROM ($DC66, $DC61, etc.), intercalando leitura de dados com AND de
      máscaras para desenhar paredes e teto.
      • Savior (kernel.asm:453-525):
      Utiliza PF por tile-row (12 scanlines por tile), onde PF0, PF1 e PF2 são escritos apenas uma vez no topo do tile (.Row) e o TIA mantém o registrador persistente
      durante o loop de 12 scanlines (.Line), que cuida puramente dos sprites GRP0 e GRP1. O kernel de Savior é muito mais simples e direto estruturalmente.
  • Física e Movimento do Jogador:
      • H.E.R.O.: Tem rotinas complexas e não-lineares de inércia, colisão por hardware TIA e lógica vertical acoplada ao motor de tela.
      • Savior (kernel.asm:110-115):
      Implementa um modelo limpo de física 16-bit com ponto fixo subpixel (vyLo, vyHi, PlayerYSub), gravidade constante (GRAVITY = $0008), acumulador de empuxo linear
      (JetPower) e verificação de colisões por bounding boxes / retângulos contra o mapa da sala (PlayerHitsMap).
  • Posicionamento Horizontal (SetObjectXPos):
      • H.E.R.O. (hero_bank0.asm#L16-L40):
      Usa um loop de divisão por 15 baseado em rotações aritméticas e cálculo inline de deslocamento fino:
        SBC #$0F / BCS ... / EOR #$0F / ASL / ASL / ASL / ASL / ADC #$80 / STA RESP0,X / STA WSYNC / STA HMP0,X

      • Savior (kernel.asm:2021-2031):
      Usa a técnica clássica de Andrew Davie (tutorial do AtariAge, sessão 24), baseada em lookup table alinhada em página (fineAdjustTable em $FF00), economizando
      ciclos em relação ao algoritmo matemático de H.E.R.O.
  • Estrutura de Memória e ROM (Bankswitching):
      • H.E.R.O.: Cartucho de 8K com bankswitching F8 (2 bancos de 4K, hot spots $1FF8 / $1FF9).
      • Savior: Cartucho de 16K com bankswitching F6 (4 bancos de 4K, hot spots $1FF6 a $1FF9). Além disso, toda a alocação de Zero-Page de Savior tem layout próprio e
      documentado em seg.u Variables.

  ──────
  ### 2. Onde há inspiração direta ou cópia das especificações/técnicas

  • Power Bar (Barra de Energia / Tempo):
      • Foi feita engenharia reversa visual e de comportamento do H.E.R.O. (inclusive documentado em power_bar_plan.md). O Savior busca ter o mesmo comportamento:
      margens cinzas via PF0=$E0, corpo contínuo via PF1/PF2=$FF, troca de cor no meio do scanline (amarelo para vermelho) e uma separação suave de 1 pixel usando a Ball
      (ENABL).
  • Display de Vidas e Bombas:
      • Uso do truque de cópias NUSIZ (3 cópias espaçadas/médias para vidas e bombas) em vez de escrever texto ou criar dígitos, espelhando a convenção de H.E.R.O.
  • Score (Pontuação):
      • Em vez da tabela PF de H.E.R.O., Savior usou no banco 1 o algoritmo clássico de 48-pixel sprite multiplexer (conhecido da comunidade homebrew).
  • Parâmetros e Regras do Mundo:
      • Paredes simétricas com CTRLPF espelhado (D0=1).
      • Dimensões de tela e áreas visíveis (144 scanlines de caverna + 48 de HUD).
      • Limites de tela do herói (PLAYER_MIN_X = 4, PLAYER_MAX_X = 163).

  ──────
  ### Conclusão

  Não é cópia do código binário ou assembly. Nenhuma sub-rotina do binário de H.E.R.O. foi extraída e colocada no Savior intacta. É uma re-implementação "clean-room" com
  engenharia reversa comportamental: o autor estudou o comportamento do jogo original no Stella e no disassembly, aprendeu as limitações do TIA que a Activision resolveu
  na época, e escreveu código 6502 novo em cima dos mesmos conceitos.
